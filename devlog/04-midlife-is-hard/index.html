I could beat my own game standing still. So I hired a robot.

Nov 01, 2026 V. 1.24 to 1.27
▶ Play this build

HHere's an embarrassing thing to admit about your own game: four minutes in, I could put the controller down.

My weapons were doing so much damage that I could stand in one spot and let midlife come to me. Bills, robocalls, donuts, all of it melting before it got close. Nineteen minutes felt like the right length for a run. The run itself just wasn't a fight.

That's backwards. In Vampire Survivors you die a lot early on, buy a few upgrades, and come back a little stronger. A first-time player should not get anywhere near the end.

So Claude hired a robot

I didn't know how to measure "too easy," so Claude built a little bot that plays the game.1It's not a smart bot. A real person lasts about three to four times longer than it does, so its numbers are a floor, not a forecast. It dodges enemies, walks toward gems and picks perks at random. Then it ran three bots at the same time on a fast-forwarded clock: a brand-new player, a half-upgraded one and a fully upgraded one.

The numbers were rough. A fully upgraded bot standing completely still lasted 13 minutes. One run banked over 5 million coins, which buys the entire shop in one go. My weapons were killing enemies faster than the game could spawn them.

The fix: the game grows with you

Now enemies get tougher and more numerous every time you level up. Bosses scale with your level and your age. Hits hurt more after 48. Coins come in slowly, so the 401(k) Shop is something you work toward over a lot of runs.

Leveling up keeps you even. Permanent upgrades are how you get ahead. Here's what that looks like in the actual file. Every one of these numbers is a dial I can turn:

Then I got a second opinion

I asked Grok to review the difficulty too.2Grok's notes came back as a document I handed straight to Claude. Two AIs passing notes through me, like a group project where I'm the only one who shows up to meetings. It came back with a full handoff document: bugs (one perk claimed to boost "all weapons" and quietly skipped three of them), tuning numbers, and some new ideas. Claude worked through it in order.

Two of those ideas made it in. Tax Bracket is an optional difficulty you can crank from "Standard Deduction" up to "Full Audit" for bigger rewards. And my favorite, the Comfort Zone Tax: go 45 seconds without getting hit and the game sends extra enemies for 20 seconds.

I played it at 1 AM with newborn twins asleep down the hall. It feels a lot more like Vampire Survivors now. I died. I wanted to go again.

What changed
