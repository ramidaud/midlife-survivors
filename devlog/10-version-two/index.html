V2 means this is starting to feel real. Somewhere between the 3 a.m. feedings, Election Day, doctor's appointments and keeping the house tidy, this thing I've been thinking about for months is actually taking shape, and it feels good.

If the kid version of me, building websites on GeoCities, could see what I built today, he wouldn't believe it. I remember what the internet was like when people's passions were websites, blogs and communities. I want to go back to that.

This started out as a way to deal with being off work. I'm not very good at taking time off, and I've been told over and over not to work on work while I'm on paternity leave. So I'm putting my energy into something I want to see go out into the world. Kinda like the twins.

Then and now

THEN AND NOW

The title screen

I love the new title screen. It ties the game to the website and makes them feel like a single unit. I love all the key art, but these are my favorites, because it's starting to feel more personal. Like a part of me is coming to life in the game.

Four of them had been retired from the title screen because the menus covered the characters. I brought them back, so the slideshow has nine scenes now.

Clocking in

Runs feel good. The lights react to your weapons now, the office is full of people, and TJ and Derek show up mid-run. But I'm worried it might still be too hard for new players. That's where I need help from playtesters and feedback. Join the Discord! The next post will go into this a little more.

The exit interview

When you die now, HR sits you down for an exit interview, and whatever got you stands there looking smug about it.

And at 63.5 the Gold Watch shows up. You can't kill it. Every hit just winds the clock back a little, and all you can do is buy time until you clock out at 65. The idea came from the Grim Reaper in Vampire Survivors. Just a little less dark. Kinda.

GOTCHA!

The interns don't just walk up anymore. TJ and Derek show up with a coffee, a burrito or a bundt cake, and you have to chase them down. Catch one and the game slows for a beat, a GOTCHA! card stamps in with their portrait, and a little reel spins to land on your treat: a heal, a shield and a bonus. The coworkers at their desks got real bodies too, with chairs, legs and their own little habits.

Clock in anywhere

Your employee file can follow you now. Sign in with Google once, and your coins, unlocks, achievements and even a run in progress move between your phone, tablet and laptop. It's optional. Your file still saves on your device either way, and there are new Privacy and Terms pages in plain words.

Twice the gear

There are 13 new things to pick up: seven weapons and six passives. Mom's Spaghetti heals you while you stand in it, noise-cancelling headphones block hits, a rolling office chair bowls through crowds, Reply-All chains from enemy to enemy, Out-of-Office slows everything near you, sticky notes trap whatever steps on them, and Thermostat Wars freezes the room. But you only get six weapon slots and six passive slots, so you have to pick a play style: Healing, Shield, Offense or Defense. Chests only upgrade what you already carry now, and if a level-up only offers something new, you can skip it and heal a little instead.

Retirement is a party

Make it to 65 and the Gold Watch stops. A clock-out station appears with balloons and presents. Walk up, punch out, and you're retired. It used to be a reaper. Now it's a party.

The office filled in

The walls aren't bare anymore. Every room has shelves, clocks, posters and its own clutter, and the coworkers at their desks sit, stand and fidget. Bosses that charge into a wall bounce off it now (BONK) and need a second to shake it off.

A few fixes too. If your graphics drop out, the game saves your run and offers a reload instead of going white. Group Chats only shoot at you from where you can see them. The office uses about a third less graphics memory, and the title screen scrolls properly on short laptop windows.

While I was writing this

The game kept going. Full Life (40 to 100) got its own bosses: the HOA President charges with a yardstick, the Retirement Audit stamps out rings of forms, and the 6 AM Pickleball Champ lunges in loops. At 100, Father Time waits to shake your hand. Sticky Notes actually stick now. And there's a new Sprint mode, like Hyper in Vampire Survivors: beat the Midlife Crisis V8 to unlock it, and the years fly by twice as fast for 50% more 401(k).

Next time: the game got a Discord

I still don't totally get Discord, but my students say it's the place to be. So the game has its own server now, and it posts its own updates: a changelog every evening, new memos when they go up, and play stats every morning. The next memo is about how that works. Come say hi.

Who helped

Derek, TJ and Ben have really been into this project. Their playthroughs and thoughtful feedback helped earn them spots in the game. My oldest son, Abdel, is working on music for the game, too. This isn't just an AI project. It's becoming a truly collaborative experience.

The AI crew did their parts: ChatGPT drew the art, Gemini wrote a lot of the lines, Grok ran the balance numbers, Fable reviewed the whole build, and Claude put it together with me.

A special shout-out to my newborn twins, my beautiful wife and my super talented son. They're the real inspiration behind this project.
