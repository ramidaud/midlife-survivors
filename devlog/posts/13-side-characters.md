There's a David Byrne song called My Madness that I can't stop playing right now.^[It's from his record with Ghost Train Orchestra. Put it on while you read this.] It feels like a conversation with yourself about learning to steer your own madness. Whatever your thing is: your emotional regulation, your ism, your neurodivergence. We all have something, and we all need to learn how to steer it, especially as we get older.

I think that's the whole game, honestly. Midlife Survivors is about steering your madness. And music is how I steer mine.

## The school run

Every morning I drive my son to school and we listen to music together. We talk. It's one of my favorite parts of the day, and then we do it all again on the way home.

Somewhere in those car rides I started noticing Abe's interest in music turning into something real. He taught himself to read music without any formal training. He plays guitar and keyboard, and he picked up the clarinet for the middle school band. I'm proud of him, and I want him to keep going, at least for his own creative satisfaction.

I know a lot of people will find this relatable. Music has been the soundtrack to my life, and I come back to it over and over, whether that's a playlist I made a year ago or my favorite album from middle school. But the emotional part is what interests me most. Music comforts me when I'm sad. It drives me when I'm focused. It gives me joy when I need it, when other things in the world aren't offering any.

So when I said last time that music is one of the most important side characters in this game, I meant it's been one of the most important side characters in my life, and I want the game to feel that way too.

## The unofficial soundtrack

Which brings me to the playlist. There's a [Midlife Survivors playlist on Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/playlist/midlife-survivors/pl.u-D8mPuala4BW), and some of these songs *are* the game to me.

**Paradise** by Harachan is pure joy and chaos. It's upbeat and silly but it still takes itself seriously, and I love music that has its tongue firmly in cheek without being a joke. Before I started building the game this song was a small obsession. I shared it with my coworkers, and Abe and I listened to it almost every day. Then I watched the video. It's a bunch of nearly-retirement-age Japanese office workers putting on little skits.^[I didn't plan that. I want to make a reel someday with our characters doing the same bit.] It just fits.

**Appreciate Ya** by Nick Shoulders is about gratitude for the help you get at every stage of life. That's been my story, and it's this project too. Every character in the game is someone who helped me, and I'm thinking of friends like Dave Foster and Darvin Graham who've been there long before any of this. And Mary and Beth Theobald, twins I grew up with in Lakewood, who have been a reassuring source of guidance and comfort for as long as I can remember. I hope my twins have a relationship like theirs when they're older.

**The Ones** by Tune-Yards radiates love for the people who stand with you. We just had twins, and the support from coworkers, friends and family has been overwhelming. I felt weird even asking people to look at the baby registry. Then they did, and we got more than we did for our first. We have almost everything we need. I can't express how thankful I am.

**Freaking Out** by Michigander, because I'm grateful and happy and also, yes, freaking out a little. Trying to keep everything together, keep myself busy, and make sure my wife and kids have what they need. The game has been a pretty good place to put that freak-out energy. It turns it into something real.

**The Future Is a Foreign Land** by Ghost is a song pretending to be written in 1969 about a scary far-off future, which happens to be right now. The future it was afraid of turned out to be an ordinary Tuesday. I like it because it says the two things I feel at once: the world might burn down, and if it does I'm going to hold the people I love for the minute it takes. That's about as midlife as it gets. Also, growing old together is the whole point of the game.

**Drowning in Your Heart** by Michael Kiwanuka is barely a month old and I already can't stop playing it. It's just his voice, a guitar and a choir, and it's about watching someone you love drown in their own heart and not being able to pull them out. It's beautiful and a little devastating.

Yes, Nine to Five is on there. Of course it is.

If you want the origin story, **Once Around the Block** by Badly Drawn Boy is the song from my high school years that got me into most of what I listen to now. Put that at one end and My Madness at the other, and that's about twenty-five years of me.

## The honest part

The game has music in it now, and I like where it's at. Ben told me he liked the original tracks better. I'm not sure I've got it dialed in yet. The truth is I know what I like, I'm just not sure how to get there. I want it slightly cacophonous but still joyful and energetic, something that sits under all the sound effects without fighting them.
