## I really liked Fifth-Grade Math Homework

I love the style of each of the bosses quite a bit. I feel like taking the time to come up with different bosses with different attacks has been pretty effective, and fun. I still think most of the time they're a little bit too easy, and might need a little bit more in terms of animation effects during the fights. But for now, I'm really enjoying when they pop up on screen.

Math Homework is from helping my son. And I think fifth grade was about the time where I had to start doing a little bit of homework myself to be able to help him.^[If that sounds familiar, you're not alone. Bob Cunningham at Understood.org has a short piece on why the math our kids learn now feels so unfamiliar to parents. Link below.]

His attack is called Pop Quiz. Dotted lines fan out first, then a spread of paper airplanes flies down them. Stand in a gap. Below half health he throws two fans and starts lobbing crumpled pages at you.

## I kind of want one

The boss closest to real life is maybe the 30-Year Mortgage. Not because I have one, but because I kind of want one. Still working on getting our first home with the white picket fence. So it's really more of a manifestation of me trying to get to the point where I can have a home of my own.

For a while the Mortgage was more of a nuisance than a fight. It comes out of the strip mall across town, and it used to take about two minutes to walk to your house while the swarm piled up. Every Maple Hollow run that got past 50 ended between 52 and 56 with the Mortgage still alive. Now bosses that are far away hurry over, and fight at normal speed once they reach you. After the fix it felt good. Challenging.

## Choose the best one

I work up the visual concept and then I think about what their attack will be. Working with Claude, I come up with a couple of different options on how that could work, choose the best one, and go from there.

The attack always comes with a warning first: dotted lines, a raised yardstick, a clipboard that flashes. Game designers call that a telegraph. Mike Stout's [Enemy Attacks and Telegraphing](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/enemy-attacks-and-telegraphing) on Game Developer explains why it matters: you can't dodge something you didn't know was coming, so a fair hit is one you had a clear chance to see.
