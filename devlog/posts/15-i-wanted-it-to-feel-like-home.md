I wanted it to feel like home. This whole game is about partially me dealing with my own anxieties. And having a little slice of home in the game is a fun feature to share. Especially with Abe.

So Maple Hollow, the game's second stage, now has our real house in it. Both floors, the garage, the stairs, and Abe's room upstairs.

## He's been thrilled

Abe has seen his room. He's been thrilled that it's in the game.

![Abe's room in the game: his name on the wall, the guitar, the music sign, the cars and games, a green beanbag in the corner.](img/15-abe-room.jpg)

## Just lose the TV

The house started from our real floor plan and photos of our rooms. The photos stay private. What you see is new pixel art, painted by Codex from those references.

Then I laid the rooms out myself. Claude built me a little House Layout Editor where I could drag, resize, turn and leave notes on every piece of furniture. My layouts went from there straight into the game.

There was one rule the furniture kept breaking. Every path the hero needs has to be at least 96 pixels wide, so a swarm can't trap you behind the couch. At the sizes I picked, the living room furniture blocked every route through the room. Pieces got moved and shrunk over three rounds, and in the end I told Claude to just lose the TV.

![Before and after of the living room: an empty room with a big TV, then the new room with the couches, the rug, the fireplace and the racing sim.](img/15-before-living.jpg "compare: img/15-before-living.jpg | img/15-living-after.jpg | Living room")

The house isn't a hiding spot. A few enemies follow you through the door, and bosses always do.

## I don't feel like it's there yet

I'm still struggling with the stage layout for the game. I'm not really quite able to put what's in my head into the code. So there's a lot of revisions. Some things are working really well, other things aren't quite right. A lot of it has to do with perspective and how things are connected in the stages.

I really like the houses and the yards in Maple Hollow. My house is coming together, but I still don't feel like it meets any sort of quality bar for a pixel art game. The office in general, I think, needs a lot of work. But I'm not quite sure how to approach these changes. Do I need to stop and figure it all out, or do I keep making small revisions?

When I asked Claude, the answer was: don't stop everything, but stop spreading revisions everywhere. Push one room, like the living room, all the way to the quality bar, then match the rest of the house to it. That's how the yards came together.

If you're curious how pixel artists think about rooms like these, Raymond Schlitter's [Pixelblog on top-down interiors](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2021/11/30/pixelblog-35-top-down-interiors) is a good place to start. He argues that the cozy feeling of pixel art comes partly from the art form itself, not just nostalgia.

## A little bit like Kent

Other than the house, none of Maple Hollow is from real life. But I did want it to feel a little bit like Kent: the fall trees, the college town vibe, the town square. The community feel.

The town opens up as you age, a little like the office promotions. The woods around your street give way to a new part of town every few years.

![The Maple Hollow map: your street in one corner, every other part of town marked with the age it opens.](img/15-town-map.jpg)

- **44:** the elementary school
- **47:** Birch Circle
- **50:** the strip mall
- **53:** the town square
- **55:** the community center
- **59:** the parkway
- **62:** Maple Park

The town square is my nod to downtown Kent. Ohio Magazine's [Best Hometowns 2022 piece on Kent](https://ohiomagazine.com/ohio-life/article/best-hometowns-2022-kent) has a good picture of what I mean by the college town vibe.^[Fun fact from that article: Acorn Alley, downtown Kent's little pocket of shops, is named for the city's black squirrels.]

## I hope they want to explore

I hope people take the time to find all the little Easter eggs. And read all the memos, and find something to laugh about or relate to.

Maple Hollow opens once you reach 55 in the office. Then pick it under Location on the title screen, find the spare key, and let yourself in.

One more piece I'd recommend if you're interested in why home spaces feel good in games: Tanya X. Short's [Designing for Coziness](https://medium.com/kitfox-games/designing-for-coziness-d33d2519a59e), from Kitfox Games. Her point that a cozy space works best as a refuge from something outside fits a game where the swarm is always coming up the street.
