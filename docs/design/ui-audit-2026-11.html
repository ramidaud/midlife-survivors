UI/UX PASS · RE:Where should I be looking?

Midlife Survivors UI audit

I ran V. 2.25.0 on a phone, an iPad both ways, and a desktop, and photographed 60 surfaces at each size. Each piece is well made. They were built one at a time, so each one chose its own spot, shape and volume, and nothing decides which one gets to speak. That is why it is hard to know where to look.

How to read this page

  • One row per surface. Left to right: phone, iPad portrait, iPad landscape, desktop.
  • Tap any picture to enlarge it.
  • Look is type, color, shape. Attention is what fights for your eyes. Device fit is how it adapts.
  • The ranked top 10 is at the end, with links back to the evidence.

Worth keeping

  • The lower third. One bar, color-coded label, already queued. It should become the main voice.
  • The cream memo strip and the devlog's reading sizes.
  • The Spotlight band. It has the most character. It needs a smaller job.
  • The level-up cards and the bonus chest as a full-screen moment.
  • The title key art and one big PUNCH IN on a phone.

How it was captured

  • Headless Chromium against the repo at V. 2.25.0 (build 327), with ?nostats=1. Stats, sign-in and the leaderboard were blocked. Nothing was posted.
  • A test save with coins, unlocks and 14 runs, so returning-player screens show.
  • In-run states were called through the game's own functions. The age 45 row is the untouched game.
  • No game code was changed.

The front door

Title screen, first visit and returning

01

Title screen, first visit

Look
The strongest screen in the game. Key art, cream memo strip, pixel logo and one orange button all agree with each other.
Attention
On a phone the eye lands on PUNCH IN, which is right. On desktop PUNCH IN moves inside the middle tile and competes with two other tiles, a row of five links, and the site nav above.
Device fit
Three different layouts for one screen. Phone stacks, iPad portrait stacks with the art cropped, desktop becomes a three-tile dashboard. The type stays phone-sized on iPad and desktop, so the tiles read as fine print.
02

Title screen, returning player

Look
The employee file adds four kinds of pill (STATUS, MEMO, TO-DO, stat boxes) in sizes from 8 to 13 px. The devlog card is cream paper, the other tiles are navy. Two paper colors side by side.
Attention
Six things ask for a tap before PUNCH IN: the version line, the feed, MORE 1/4, the account row, the devlog card, the settings chip. A returning player wants one thing: play again.
Device fit
Phone needs a full scroll to reach the shop and options. On iPad portrait the title art is covered by the stack after one screen.
03

Shift settings (location, mode, sprint, tax)

Look
Four rows of small pill buttons with emoji and locks, each followed by a line of help text. It reads like a settings form, and nothing else in the game looks like it.
Attention
This is where you pick the stage, which is one of the biggest choices before a run. It hides behind a small gray chip under PUNCH IN.
Device fit
Opens inline and pushes everything down on phone. On desktop it grows inside the middle tile and shoves PUNCH IN upward, so the main button moves when you open it.
04

What's New bubble

Look
A fourth container style on the title: a navy sheet with a small orange tag. Not the memo strip, not a tile.
Attention
Fine as a one-time nudge. On phone it covers the employee picker, bottom of screen. On desktop it sits top right. Two homes for one message.
Device fit
Bottom sheet on phone and iPad, floating card on desktop. That split is actually the right instinct and worth keeping in the system.
05

Title screen, Maple Hollow picked

Look
The title art swaps to the house, which is a lovely touch. Nothing else on the screen says which stage you picked.
Attention
After you choose Maple Hollow the only confirmation is the picture. The settings chip still reads Midlife, 40-65, Tax 0 with no location in it.
Device fit
Same layout notes as the title.
06

Save your file prompt

Look
Cleanest dialog in the game: one strip, one headline, one primary and one secondary button. Its strip says HR // BADGE OFFICE, a third naming pattern next to INTERNAL MEMO and FORM 401-K.
Attention
It appears on its own over the title for players with progress. Good moment, but it blocks PUNCH IN until answered. (The red error line in these shots is my test rig blocking the network.)
Device fit
Centered card at every size. On a phone a bottom sheet would put both buttons under the thumb.
07

We Moved note

Look
Same card as the account prompt, with MEMO // FACILITIES on the strip. Here the orange button is on the right and the outline button on the left. The account prompt stacks them.
Attention
One-time notice. No issue.
Device fit
Fine.

Menus from the title

Shop, extras, records, options, archives

08

401(k) Shop

Look
Every row carries a full-width orange BUY button, so the screen has eight primary buttons. Pips are text squares. Locked rows use a gray that isn't in the palette.
Attention
Your coin balance, the one number this screen is about, is a line of small text under the heading. The game logo and tagline repeat above it and take the best spot.
Device fit
One column on phone (two items visible), two on iPad, four on desktop. The grid adapts well. The panel header does not: logo, tagline and strip cost about 150 px on every size.
09

Extras

Look
A poll, a start-age picker, and eight unrelated buttons (Lore, Patch Notes, Credits, Achievements, Discord, Reset Progress, Art Room, Back) in one grid. Reset Progress sits next to Art Room with only a red outline to mark it.
Attention
No order of importance. A destructive button shares a row with links that leave the game.
Device fit
Buttons stack one per row on phone, which makes this a long scroll of same-looking bars.
10

Performance record (achievements)

Look
Tiles are consistent with each other. Earned tiles get an orange border, locked ones dim. This is close to a reusable card already.
Attention
55 tiles with 10 px descriptions. Hard to see what's close to done.
Device fit
2, 3 and 5 columns. Works. Text is too small on iPad and desktop, where there is room to grow.
11

Leaderboard

Look
The table uses 8 to 9 px Silkscreen headers. The Back button is the largest thing on the panel.
Attention
The panel is a small box over busy key art, and on desktop most of the screen is the swarm picture behind it. (Offline here because the test blocks the network, so the empty state is what you see.)
Device fit
The panel does not use the height it has on iPad or desktop.
12

Credits

Look
All Silkscreen caps, including names. The One Look rule says Atkinson for anything you read.
Attention
Fine. It is a list.
Device fit
One column on phone, four on desktop. Works.
13

Options and controls

Look
A different design language from the title panels: Atkinson bold caps heading, green READY pills, arrow steppers, a teal reset button. The strip reads ALL 4 INPUT DEVICES ACTIVE // AUTO-SAVED TO LOCALSTORAGE and rows say (BTN 0). That is developer language.
Attention
The screen opens on input remapping. Sound, music and game tips, the things most players come here for, live in the pause menu instead.
Device fit
Long single column on phone, two columns on iPad and desktop. Steppers are about 20 px tall on a phone, well under a comfortable tap.
14

What's New (patch notes)

Look
Third heading style (Atkinson bold caps with an emoji). The orange button label includes its own key list: BACK TO MENU (SPACE / ENTER / ESC / A).
Attention
Readable. The newest entry is boxed in orange, good.
Device fit
Same card at every size, centered. Fine on desktop, tight on phone.

Lore archive

Fine for a reading screen.

Same as patch notes.

The HUD

What is always on screen

20

HUD, early run

Look
Three type sizes in one corner (12, 11, 10 px), a 22 px timer, a 15 px orange age. Empty loadout slots are 12 gray squares with no labels.
Attention
Calm, which is right for the first minutes. The age is the number that matters (it is the goal) and it is smaller than the timer.
Device fit
On iPad portrait the HUD is the phone HUD, slightly enlarged, on a much bigger screen. The camera also zooms out, so the hero is tiny. Pause and sound buttons are 36 px on phone, under the 44 px touch minimum.

Device fit
It sits 57 px lower than its own CSS asks for, on every size. The site nav hides during a run but its height variable stays at 57 px, so everything positioned from it sits low.

Top 10 problems

Ranked by how much each one hurts "where should I be looking"

  1. 1

    One moment speaks through three or four channels at once.

    A boss arrives with a card, a speech bubble, a tip strip, a center flash and often an alert. A modifier fires a flash, a lower third and a chip. A birthday is world text twice, a lower third and a flash. Nothing decides who talks.

    See: Boss card, first attack and an alert together Modifier starts The real age 45 moment Promotion card

  2. 2

    In-run messages have no single home.

    The same kind of news can land in the center, the bottom, the top, a side chip or a full band. Tips alone have two homes. Your eyes have to search for where the game chose to speak this time.

    See: Center flash Lower third Tip card Errand starts

  3. 3

    The busiest reading happens exactly when you need to dodge.

    The boss card dims the floor where the first attack is drawn, and on a phone the flash and the errand strip sit in the same band as the hero.

    See: Boss arrival card Boss card, first attack and an alert together Errand strip and arrow

  4. 4

    Chips and the minimap draw on top of menus.

    Level-ups, chests and shops open underneath a live errand strip, its arrow and the minimap. They only hide for the pause menu.

    See: Level-up with a live errand

  5. 5

    Chips collide with each other on a phone.

    The buff chip, modifier chip, errand strip, minimap and (in Overtime) the Workload chip each picked their own spot and shape. Several have no phone rule at all.

    See: Modifier chip Overtime stack Checked off buff

  6. 6

    Everything in a run sits 57 px too low.

    The site nav hides during a run but its height keeps being added to every position. That pushes the minimap and chips toward the hero on every device. A one-line fix.

    See: HUD with minimap

  7. 7

    The interface does not grow with the screen.

    iPad and desktop get phone-sized type (8 to 13 px) with more empty space around it. On iPad portrait the camera zooms out too, so the hero and all world text shrink.

    See: HUD, early run Title screen, first visit Speech bubble and damage numbers Leaderboard

  8. 8

    There is no one card, one button, one chip.

    During a run a message can arrive in 11 different containers. Menus use more than 10 different strip headings and at least 5 pill styles. In the code: 30 font sizes, 12 corner radii, 29 stacking layers, 13 screen-width breakpoints, and 16 files that inject their own styles.

    See: Options and controls Bonus chest Modifier chip Shift briefing and hire card

  9. 9

    Most screens have several primary buttons, or none.

    Eight orange BUY buttons in the shop, three TAP TO SELECT on level-up, Post Score stacked on Quick Retry. On the desktop title PUNCH IN is one of three tiles.

    See: 401(k) Shop Level-up Score and leaderboard on game over Title screen, returning player

  10. 10

    Settings and identity are scattered.

    Sound and tips are in Pause, controls are in Options, stage and mode hide behind a chip on the title. Sub-menus repeat the game logo and tagline at the top of every panel. Options and patch notes use developer wording (LOCALSTORAGE, BTN 0).

    See: Pause Options and controls Shift settings (location, mode, sprint, tax) 401(k) Shop

What I could not capture

  • The Workload chip. It only draws in a real Overtime run. Its style is read from the code.
  • Phone landscape. Not in the brief's four sizes. The spec will cover it.
  • Real notches and home bars. Headless browsers have no safe areas. That needs your phone.
  • Controller focus on every menu. I checked the pause menu only.
  • The live leaderboard and share pages (network blocked on purpose).
